Meadow Maps is a guide for community organizers who need a consistent way to describe local walking routes.

> Fictional teaching fixture. The project, sponsor, maintainers, and funding relationship are invented for instruction. All labeled fixture destinations are reserved examples. See the [fixture contract](README.md#teaching-fixtures).

## 💎 Sponsors & Partners

The fictional **Northstar Cooperative** funds translation of this guide into additional community languages. Its assumed agreement permits this public acknowledgment.

Learn about the [translation sponsor (fixture)](https://example.org/northstar). This recognition describes an assumed existing relationship; it is separate from the voluntary maintainer support below.

## ✨ Features

- A short, repeatable format for route descriptions.
- Explicit dates for observations that can change over time.
- Space to record known access barriers without guessing accessibility.

## 🚀 Getting Started

No installation is required. Choose a route you have recently walked and create a note containing its start point, distance, surface, known barriers, and observation date.

Check the note against your own observations before sharing it. Avoid including private addresses or claiming that an unchecked route is accessible to everyone.

## 📖 Usage

Use this illustrative entry as a guide to content and order:

```text
Route: Riverside Loop
Start: Public north entrance
Distance: 1.2 km
Surface: Paved path with a short gravel section
Known barriers: Three steps at the southern exit
Observed: 2026-09-15
```

The route and observations are fictional. For a real entry, give verified facts and explain uncertainty. When conditions change, update both the description and the observation date.

Do not remove a known access barrier solely to make the description shorter.

## 🤝 Contributing
